# Institutional transition plan

## Purpose

This plan enables Kenya Data Atlas to move from an independent private-citizen project to an NGO, multilateral, foundation, university, media organization or other public-interest institution without losing provenance, independence or audit history.

UNDP is an example of a possible future institutional home, not a current sponsor, partner or committed buyer. The Atlas must not use UN or UNDP names, emblems or implied endorsement before written authorization and an approved public announcement.

## Transition principles

- No ownership, endorsement or partnership claim precedes a signed agreement.
- Historical sources, releases, vintages and audit records transfer intact.
- The acquiring organization cannot silently rewrite historical decisions.
- Public licences and third-party restrictions continue to apply after transfer.
- Personal credentials are replaced with institutional identities and managed secrets.
- Public users receive clear notice of the new operator, effective date and policy changes.
- Editorial and statistical independence terms are recorded explicitly.

## Due diligence package

The Owner prepares:

- repository, domain and deployment inventory;
- source and licensing register;
- data and boundary inventories;
- provenance and quality reports;
- open corrections, security issues and known limitations;
- third-party dependencies and contracts;
- intellectual-property ownership evidence;
- privacy and security assessments;
- operating costs, release calendar and roadmap;
- credentials/secrets inventory without exposing secret values;
- contributor and reviewer agreements where applicable.

## Institutional acceptance checklist

- [ ] Signed asset/IP transfer, licence or operating agreement.
- [ ] Approved public description of the relationship.
- [ ] Institutional accountable executive and product owner appointed.
- [ ] Statistical, data, geography, privacy, engineering and editorial roles appointed.
- [ ] Conflicts and editorial-independence rules approved.
- [ ] Source licences reassessed for change of operator where necessary.
- [ ] Privacy controller/processor roles documented.
- [ ] Infrastructure, domains, repositories and accounts transferred securely.
- [ ] Credentials rotated and personal access removed or reduced appropriately.
- [ ] Backups and restoration tested by the new operator.
- [ ] Open issues, corrections and risks accepted by named owners.
- [ ] Public operator, privacy, terms and contact notices updated.
- [ ] Transition decision and effective timestamp preserved in the audit log.

## Governance upgrade

An institutional owner should replace the sole-publisher model with separated approval duties:

```text
Preparer → Statistical reviewer → Privacy/licensing review where needed → Publisher
```

It may establish a Governance Board or steering committee for methodology, disputes and high-risk modelling. The independent-era policies remain the baseline until the institution approves versioned replacements.

## Failed or incomplete transfer

If negotiations end without a signed agreement, ownership and control remain with the private-citizen Owner. Draft branding, credentials and confidential diligence material are withdrawn, and neither party is described publicly as a partner or owner.
